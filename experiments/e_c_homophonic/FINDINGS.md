# Family C — homophonic solving

**Verdict: no configuration beats its matched surrogate baseline. The maximum
z-score across all eighteen configurations, against the null that controls length
and symbol inventory exactly, is −0.13 — i.e. the real corpus never once exceeds
even the *mean* of the noise distribution, let alone the 3-sigma bar. Family C
is a calibrated negative.**

## The rule this family is run under

An AZdecrypt-style solver *always* returns something that reads like words. The
only threshold that means anything is the distribution of the identical solver's
best-of-restarts score on surrogates. Zodiac-340 cleared its baseline by many
sigma. **Anything under 3 sigma here is noise and is reported as noise, however
good the output looks.** Every configuration below is reported with its
surrogate baseline and with the digits of pi run through the same pipeline,
side by side.

## Method

`solver.py`: symbol -> letter map over the 27-symbol alphabet `a-z` + space,
searched by simulated annealing (geometric temperature schedule, random symbol
order, `exp(delta/T)` acceptance) followed by a deterministic best-improvement
polish, best of N random restarts. Scoring is character 5-gram log-probability,
reported as **log10 P per plaintext character**. Incremental scoring is
numba-jitted: reassigning one symbol only rescores the 5-gram windows touching
that symbol's positions, with overlapping windows merged so each index is counted
once. The delta was verified exact against a full rescore (30/30 random moves,
`|error| < 1e-3`).

## Language models

CipSoft is German and this is 2001 content, so German is a first-class
hypothesis. Three models were built (`build_lm.py`):

| model | alphabet |
|---|---|
| `en` | English |
| `de_ae` | German, umlauts ASCII-folded `ä→ae`, `ö→oe`, `ü→ue`, `ß→ss` |
| `de_a` | German, cruder folding `ä→a`, `ö→o`, `ü→u`, `ß→ss` |

**Corpus provenance, stated plainly:** general web egress is blocked by this
session's proxy policy (403 on CONNECT to `gutenberg.org`, `wikipedia.org`,
`tibia.fandom.com`), so no running-text corpus — Wortschatz Leipzig,
Gutenberg-DE or otherwise — could be fetched. PyPI *is* reachable, and
`wordfreq` bundles the large Exquisite-Corpus frequency lists (300k word types
per language, from Wikipedia, subtitles, news, books and web text). The models
are therefore built from 20M characters synthesised by sampling word types in
proportion to their corpus frequency. **This captures within-word and
word-boundary character statistics faithfully but carries no syntax beyond word
unigrams**, so it is weaker than a running-text model. It does not bias the
comparison: the real corpus and every surrogate are scored with the identical
model.

Smoothing is interpolated absolute discounting (Kneser–Ney form, D=0.75) with a
minimum-evidence threshold: a context seen fewer than 3 times backs off
wholesale. That threshold is not cosmetic. Without it, junk word types in the
frequency list ("aaa", "zzzz") gave otherwise-unseen 5-gram contexts enough
weight to make a **constant string score better than real English**
(`P(a|aaaa) = 0.44`, giving `-0.359` per char against real text's `-0.633`) —
a degenerate attractor that would have made every Family C result worthless.
Each model is now gated on degenerate strings scoring worse than held-out text:

| model | held-out text | `aaaa…` | `   …` | `abab…` | random |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `en` | -0.633 | -4.88 | -6.01 | -2.81 | -5.12 |
| `de_a` | -0.618 | -4.88 | -6.01 | -2.81 | -5.07 |

## Solver calibration

A negative result from an unvalidated solver is worthless. The solver was run on
homophonic ciphers of real English text at the same sizes as the 469 symbol
streams (`calibrate.py`):

| plaintext chars | symbols | recovered score | true-plaintext score | char accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1200 | 60 | -0.6435 | -0.6511 | **0.999** |
| 1950 | 100 | -0.6310 | -0.6356 | **0.999** |
| 1300 | 374 | -1.177 | — | **0.092** |

The first two are the sizes of the live 469 configurations, and the solver
recovers them essentially perfectly. **The third row is a stated limitation:**
at 374 symbols over 1300 tokens (3.5 observations per symbol, 156 hapax) the
problem is not solvable by any method at this length, so `C1_triples` is
reported as **underpowered by construction**, not as a negative result.

### The two nulls disagree, and the disagreement is fully explained

Five C2 configurations clear +3 sigma against `Markov3_digits`. **That is an
artifact, and it is the single most important thing in this writeup**, because
it is exactly the kind of number that a less careful run would have reported as
a hit.

`Markov3_digits` does not control the symbol inventory. An MDL parse of a
Markov-3 *digit* surrogate produces far fewer token types than a parse of the
real corpus (45 vs 76 on the dedup core; 82 vs 121 on the contigs; 72 vs 114 on
master_v1). A homophonic solver with more symbols has more free parameters and
can always drive the 5-gram score higher, so a vocabulary gap manufactures a
positive z on its own. The evidence:

```
Pearson r(vocabulary gap, z against Markov3_digits)  = +0.908
Pearson r(vocabulary gap, z against Markov3_symbols) = +0.323
```

Where the vocabulary happens to match — `C1_pairs`, real V = 96 against a
surrogate mean of 96.3 — the digit-level z collapses to **+0.28**. Where it does
not match, z rises in proportion to the gap. The z is measuring the
tokeniser, not the corpus.

`Markov3_symbols` holds length and vocabulary exactly fixed. Against it:

```
max z over all 18 configurations = -0.13
```

**Not one of the eighteen configurations reaches even the mean of the matched null.** After
Benjamini–Hochberg across all configurations (a family testing many
configurations does not get to quote its best raw p), every q-value against the
matched null is **1.0000**.

### The output looks like words. So does the noise.

The first duty on producing plausible-looking text is to show what the same
solver produces on surrogates. Side by side, best C2_mdl / dedup_core / en:

```
real corpus     : ahas asas shhaahhhs sa a shaahs in haasa h  s a same so s as sashaa aha tsa h
Markov3 digits  :  sa naaas a as a sa anisaananaas sss anaa sa anssnaaassa ssat nasaannssnnanna
Markov3 symbols : et s she  sssts sates ss s sea stasis sts seasi s  s ssiissted shatassaaatss e
```

The symbol-level surrogate — pure noise by construction — produces "et s she
sssts sates ss s sea stasis sts seasi", which reads *more* like English than the
real corpus does, and it scores higher. This is what the surrogate-maximum
criterion is for.

### Family B's frontier band

Family B's enumeration showed that a 26–32 letter alphabet cannot produce the
observed statistics: reaching mean codeword length 1.577 needs only 4–5
single-digit codewords and hence a **~55–73 symbol** alphabet. If there is a
code here at all it is homophonic, which puts it squarely in this family's
scope, so three configurations were added with the MDL parse steered into that
band (`C2_mdlB`, `maxlen=16`): **V = 65** on the dedup core and **V = 68** on
`master_v1`.

They are the best-targeted configurations in the study and they are also,
usefully, the ones with the smallest vocabulary gap to their digit-level nulls
(7.0 symbols on master_v1, against 41.9 for `C2_mdl`). With that confound
largely removed, the digit-level z collapses to **−0.58, +1.34 and +1.44** — and
the matched-null z stays negative (−0.92 and below). The frontier band contains
no signal either.

### German versus English

German was run as a first-class hypothesis on every live configuration, in both
umlaut foldings. It makes no difference: the German models score the real
corpus within noise of the English one (e.g. dedup core C2: en -1.8542, de_ae
-1.8492, de_a -1.8778) and fail the matched null identically. There is no German
signal here, and no English one.

### Power: which rows are negatives and which are merely underpowered

A negative from an unvalidated solver is worthless, so each configuration's shape
was tested by enciphering real English at the same (length, symbol count) and
asking whether the solver recovers it (`underpowered.py`):

| shape | n | S | recovered | char accuracy | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C2_mdl / dedup_core | 1923 | 76 | -0.6305 | 0.999 | **solvable** |
| C2_mdl / contigs | 2241 | 121 | -0.6441 | 1.000 | **solvable** |
| C2_mdl / master_v1 | 2000 | 100 | -0.6350 | 1.000 | **solvable** |
| C1_pairs / dedup_core | 1946 | 96 | -0.6307 | 0.999 | **solvable** |
| C1_triples / dedup_core | 1293 | 374 | -1.1628 | 0.060 | UNDERPOWERED |
| C1_pairs / seed | 289 | 87 | -1.1033 | 0.066 | UNDERPOWERED |
| C2_mdl / seed | 554 | 12 | — | — | ILL-POSED |

So the C2 rows and `C1_pairs/dedup_core` are **genuine negatives**: had a
homophonic plaintext been there, this solver would have found it — it recovers
an injected one at 99.9–100% character accuracy at exactly those shapes.

`C1_triples` is **underpowered**, not refuted: 374 symbols over 1293 tokens is
3.5 observations per symbol, and the solver fails on a *known* cipher of that
shape. Its +5.10 against the digit-level null is doubly meaningless — an
underpowered configuration cannot produce a real hit, and it fails the matched
null at −3.05 anyway.

### The 578-digit innovation seed

Agent 1's Family E result says the copies carry no information and only the
innovation content can carry a message. Taking `data/seed_estimate.txt` (578
digits) as a first-class target, the honest answer is that **578 digits is not
enough, and worse, the natural parse of it is ill-posed**:

- **`C2_mdl` on the seed collapses to V = 12.** A homophonic cipher needs at
  least as many symbols as plaintext letters; twelve symbols cannot carry a
  26-letter alphabet under any assignment. This configuration is **ill-posed**,
  not merely weak, and no amount of compute changes that.
- **`C1_pairs` on the seed gives 289 tokens over 95 symbols** — three
  observations per symbol. The solver recovers only 6.6% of a *known* cipher at
  that shape. **Underpowered.**

Both were run to completion with full surrogate baselines anyway (they are
cheap), and both are noise. But they should be reported as *underpowered and
ill-posed*, not as evidence that the seed contains nothing. **If a message
exists in the innovation content, homophonic solving at 578 digits cannot find
it and cannot rule it out.** That is a limit of the data, not of the search.

### A note on the harness verdict field

`results/C_*.json` will show `INCOMPLETE (missing required null families)`. That
is a labelling artifact, not a defect: the harness's `DEFAULT_FAMILIES` list is
written for digit-level experiments, and Family C runs on *symbol* streams,
where the correct matched null is `Markov3_symbols` (exact length and
vocabulary) rather than the digit-level generators. The scoring in this
directory is complete; the required-family check simply does not know about
symbol-stream nulls.

## Verdict

**Family C is a calibrated negative.** On a solver demonstrated to recover
injected plaintext at 99.9–100% accuracy at exactly these shapes, across
variable-width MDL token streams and fixed-width pairs and triples, on the
deduplicated core, the contigs and the ILP-assembled master, in English and in
two German foldings, **no configuration reaches even the mean of its matched
surrogate distribution** (max z = −0.13, all BH q = 1.0000). Zodiac-340 cleared
its baseline by many sigma. This clears nothing.

The one honest caveat, stated where it belongs rather than buried: the two
configurations on the 578-digit innovation seed — the only object Family E
leaves as a candidate carrier — are underpowered and ill-posed respectively, so
Family C neither supports nor excludes a message there.
